A naive approach would be to calculate f(0),f(1),f(2),… and wait until you find a pair of consecutive terms that match the ones you were given. 
However, because the secret number is massive (derived from a text string), this search would take an astronomical amount of time and i started looking for ways to avoid this  .


 The two pieces of information i  was  given can be expressed algebraically. Lemme define them 
* $k = \text{secret} - 1$
* $x = 13^k \pmod p$
* $y = 37^k \pmod p$

Using these variables, the two values from the challenge (`remain[0]` and `remain[1]`) can be written as a **system of linear equations**:

1.  $x + y = \text{remain}[0]$
2.  $13x + 37y = \text{remain}[1]$


With two equations and two variables, we can now solve for $x$ and $y$. Using the substitution method:

1.  From the first equation, we get $x = \text{remain}[0] - y$.
2.  We substitute this into the second equation: $13(\text{remain}[0] - y) + 37y = \text{remain}[1]$.
3.  Solving this for $y$ gives: $y = (\text{remain}[1] - 13 \cdot \text{remain}[0]) \cdot 24^{-1} \pmod p$.

This required calculating the **modular inverse** of 24 modulo p. Once $y$ was found, $x$ could be calculated easily. The result of this step was knowing the actual numerical value of $13^{\text{secret}-1} \pmod p$.



###   The Discrete Logarithm Problem (DLP)

The final piece of the puzzle was to extract the exponent. After Step 2, we had an equation of the form:
$$13^k = x \pmod p$$

Here, we know the base (13), the result ($x$), and the modulus ($p$), but we need to find the exponent ($k$). This is the classic **Discrete Logarithm Problem (DLP)**. It's considered a "hard" problem in cryptography, which is why specialized algorithms are needed to solve it. We used the `sympy` library's `discrete_log` function for this.

Once the solver found $k$, the secret was revealed with $\text{secret} = k + 1$, which was then converted from a number back into the flag text.
